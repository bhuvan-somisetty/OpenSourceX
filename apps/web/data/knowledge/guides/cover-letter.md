## The four questions an LFX cover letter must answer

LFX programs ask the cover letter to answer these four questions:

1. **How did you find out about the mentorship program?**
2. **Why are you interested in this program / project?**
3. **What experience and knowledge or skills do you have that apply to this project?**
4. **What do you hope to get out of this mentorship?**

Answer each one under its own heading, using the same wording, so the reviewer can find it fast. Many strong CNCF applications then go on with a full proposal: problem, technical plan, timeline and risks (see _Writing a mentorship proposal_).

## What makes a cover letter stand out

- **Prove you have already engaged.** Link the exact issues, PRs, design docs or Slack threads you worked on. A table of "PR, what I did, why it matters for this project" works well.
- **Be specific to this project.** Name its components, files or open problems. A letter that would fit any project reads as generic.
- **Show you understand the problem.** Restate the mentorship issue in your own words and explain why it matters to the project's users.
- **Be honest about your experience and time.** Overcommitting or underselling sets you and your mentor up for a rough term. State your weekly hours and timezone, and say when your exams are.
- **Write it yourself.** Mentors see hundreds of applications and spot generic or AI-written text quickly.
- **Keep it readable:** clear headings, short paragraphs, one to three pages before any proposal sections.

## A cover letter outline that works

1. Title: program, term, project name, mentors, link to the mentorship issue.
2. Name and contact: GitHub, email, university or job, timezone.
3. How I found the project (question 1).
4. Why I want to work on this (question 2): a concrete pain point you experienced in the project.
5. Experience and skills (question 3): languages, tools, and 2–3 of your PRs with links.
6. Optional proof of concept: a draft PR or a small prototype.
7. What I hope to get out of it (question 4), plus your plan to keep contributing after the term.
8. Availability: hours per week, timezone, known conflicts.
