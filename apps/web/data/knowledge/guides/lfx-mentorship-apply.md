## Prerequisite tasks when applying to LFX Mentorship

Every LFX Mentorship application asks for the same two documents, and each project can add its own prerequisites on top:

| What                                    | Asked by        | Notes                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Resume / CV**                         | Every program   | One or two pages. Link your GitHub and your open source pull requests.                                          |
| **Cover letter** (statement of purpose) | Every program   | Answers four set questions (see _LFX cover letter_). Many CNCF mentors expect it to read like a short proposal. |
| **Project-specific prerequisites**      | Set per program | Listed in the program's **Prerequisites** section on its LFX page. Each project chooses different ones.         |

Project-specific prerequisites vary, so some programs ask for one item and others ask for several. The common ones are:

- **A certificate of completion of a free Linux Foundation Training course.** For example, the Linux Kernel Mentorship requires _A Beginner's Guide to Linux Kernel Development (LFD103)_. You finish the course and upload the certificate you download from it.
- **A coding task or test**, or a first contribution (an issue fix, a small PR), often linked from the project's GitHub mentorship issue.
- **A project proposal** with your technical plan and a week-by-week timeline. CNCF projects usually ask for it in the cover letter or as an attached document.
- **Proof of enrollment** (student ID, transcript or admission letter) for programs that are limited to students.
- **Answers to project questions**, or a link to your earlier contributions.

Some programs need only the resume and the cover letter. Others ask for all three of resume, cover letter and a certificate or proposal. Always open the program's own LFX page and read its Prerequisites section before you apply.

**Applications without every prerequisite are not accepted.** The application stays **Pending** until you upload or finish every prerequisite during the review period.

## How to apply on the LFX platform, step by step

1. Create a mentee profile at [mentorship.lfx.linuxfoundation.org](https://mentorship.lfx.linuxfoundation.org/) with your Linux Foundation account.
2. Open **Mentorships** and filter by **Accepting Applications**.
3. Open the program you want, read its description and **Prerequisites**, then click **Apply** and pick the term.
4. Upload your **resume** and **cover letter**, and finish every prerequisite (certificates, tasks, proposal).
5. Your application stays **Pending** until every prerequisite is done. After that the program admin changes its status.
6. Track the status in your LFX dashboard. Review takes at least two weeks.
7. If you are selected, you get an email (sometimes a call), then sign a contract and finish HR onboarding.

## How many LFX programs can I apply to?

You can apply to **at most three mentorship programs per term**. Programs you withdraw from during the application period do not count toward the limit, but re-applications do. You can only take part in one mentorship at a time, and an earlier LF mentorship can make you ineligible for another (see _Am I eligible?_ in the LFX docs).

## When LFX applications open

LFX runs three full-time terms a year: Term 1 March–May, Term 2 June–August and Term 3 September–November. Mentee applications open a few weeks before each term starts. CNCF posts the exact dates of every term at [github.com/cncf/mentoring](https://github.com/cncf/mentoring/tree/main/programs/lfx-mentorship). Star that repo and join **#cncf-mentoring** on the CNCF Slack ([slack.cncf.io](https://slack.cncf.io)) to see the next window. OpenSourceX lists every CNCF term on the [LFX program page](/programs/lfx).

## How LFX mentors choose mentees

Mentors read every completed application, shortlist a few, and interview only their top candidates. Fit matters more than PR count:

- **Genuine motivation.** Be ready for "why this project, not the other 100?".
- **A specific, honest application.** Show you understand what the project needs.
- **Clear communication.** It matters as much as raw skill.
- **Realistic commitment.** Full-time terms are real work, so be upfront about your hours.
- **Visible engagement.** Link the issues, PRs and docs you have already worked on.

## LFX mistakes to avoid

- Don't pester mentors about selection, in DMs, pings or email. If you're shortlisted, they reach out.
- Don't send AI-generated PRs or applications in bulk. Reviewers can tell.
- Don't wait for the application window to open. Start contributing before it.
- Don't ghost. If life gets in the way, tell your mentor.
- Don't disparage other applicants.

## If you are not selected for LFX

The programs are competitive: hundreds apply for a handful of spots. Keep contributing, apply again next term, and don't expect individual feedback, because mentors cannot give it at that volume. Ask questions in public (the #cncf-mentoring Slack channel or [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/cncf/mentoring/discussions)). For things that cannot be asked publicly, use the contact email listed in the [cncf/mentoring README](https://github.com/cncf/mentoring).
